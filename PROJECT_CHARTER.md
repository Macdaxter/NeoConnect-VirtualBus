# VirtualBus Project Charter

## Principle

VirtualBus exists to enable play, not to control access.

The complete base multiplayer experience must remain usable without artificial
feature locks. Local operation and self-hosted infrastructure must remain
possible. Official servers, hardware and support may improve convenience, but
must not replace user control over a working installation.

## Rules

1. The base experience is not artificially reduced to sell existing player,
   transport or session functionality back to users.
2. Local operation remains possible, including direct adapters, local WLAN,
   RS485, MAME, MiSTer and self-hosted relays where supported.
3. Private servers and user-owned infrastructure are permitted. No protocol is
   bound exclusively to an official service.
4. There is no hidden online requirement. Accounts may support optional public
   rooms, invitations, backups or support, but not local play.
5. Active transport, connected cabinets, versions, diagnostics and known
   limits are visible to the user.
6. Telemetry is voluntary and purpose-bound. Logs are local by default;
   automatic upload requires consent and avoids unnecessary personal data.
7. Base software is not made artificially scarce by cabinet or install count.
8. Independent transports, emulator integrations, FPGA cores, adapters,
   launchers and server implementations are not deliberately prevented.
9. Official references, compatible third-party implementations, experimental
   forks and unsupported modifications remain clearly distinguishable.
10. Monetization is transparent. Hardware, support and development work may be
    sold; dark patterns and artificial feature withholding are not accepted.
11. A beta is a responsible dialogue. The core is usable, reports are handled,
    and every request is not automatically a release requirement.
12. Preservation comes before platform lock-in. Binaries, source obligations,
    checksums, protocol versions, configuration and known limits are archived.

## Release commitment

Every release states what is proven, what is limited and what is outside its
scope. Future plans are never presented as available functionality.
